Introduction

You don't have a lead problem.

You have a leak.

Most growth advice is about getting more leads. But look at the ones you already get. Calls go to voicemail. Quotes go unchased. Happy clients never get asked. Past clients drift to whoever advertised last.

Pouring more water into a leaking bucket is the most expensive way to fill it.

An ordinary Tuesday

Four leaks before dinner.

08:47

A homeowner calls. You're on a job. Voicemail. They call the next name on Google.

11:30

A quote goes out. You mean to chase it. Then it's next month.

15:10

A client loves the finished job. Nobody asks for a review.

19:20

Last year's client needs you again. They can't remember your name, so they click an ad.

An illustrative day. The details are invented. The four moments happen in nearly every service business.

03
Leak 03 · Job done

The review nobody asked for

Plugged byThe Reputation Manager
The leak

Happy clients stay quiet. Unless you ask.

97%

of consumers use reviews to decide what to buy.5

83%

of people who were asked for a review left one.5

47%

won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.5

74%

want to see reviews from the last three months.5

Unhappy clients write reviews unprompted. Happy ones need a nudge. A business that never asks ends up with fewer, older, grumpier reviews than its work deserves.

The fix

The Reputation Manager asks everyone, at the right moment.

1

The job is done

One tap from you, or automatically from your calendar.

2

It asks how it went

A short WhatsApp message while the work is fresh. Not a survey a week later.

3

It routes the answer

Happy? Your Google link, one tap away. Unhappy? You hear first, so you can fix it.

4

It replies

Every new review gets a thoughtful reply in your voice.

Unhappy clients still get the link. Asking only happy clients is called review gating, and Google's review policy forbids it.11 So we ask everyone, and you get the chance to put things right first.
